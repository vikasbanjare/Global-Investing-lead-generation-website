Who can start investing?

  • Resident IndiansEligible resident individuals can remit under RBI’s LRS, subject to the $250,000 annual limit.
  • What you will needKeep PAN, identity and address details ready. Additional KYC and declarations may apply.
  • Who else can apply?Eligible NRIs may apply, subject to residence-country restrictions and verification.

How your INR becomes USD buying power

INR → USD
  1. Start an LRS remittance from your Indian bank
  2. Bank verifies the request and converts INR to USD
  3. Funds are routed through the GIFT City account
  4. USD buying power becomes available to invest

Transfer times depend on the bank, applicable checks and processing schedules.

How you bring your money back to India

USD → INR
  1. Request a withdrawal (minimum USD 20)
  2. Your request goes through the required review
  3. USD is converted to INR after approval
  4. INR reaches your linked Indian bank account

Indicative processing: 1–2 working days after approval; bank timelines may vary.

FAQs

Yes. Eligible resident individuals can use RBI’s Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) to invest in permitted overseas securities, subject to its $250,000 annual aggregate limit and applicable rules. Eligible NRIs may apply subject to location and onboarding restrictions.

RBI’s Liberalised Remittance Scheme generally allows eligible resident individuals to remit up to $250,000 per financial year across permitted LRS purposes. This is an overall limit, not a separate US stocks allowance.

Eligible fractional US stocks and ETFs can be purchased starting from $1, subject to the supported instrument and order rules. Bank remittance minimums, charges and foreign-exchange conversion may affect the amount you choose to transfer.

Regular US market hours are 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern Time. That is usually 7:00 PM to 1:30 AM IST during US daylight saving time, or 8:00 PM to 2:30 AM IST during US standard time, excluding market holidays.

For Indian tax residents, profits on sale and dividends may be taxable in India. Broadly, relevant long-term US share gains may fall under the 24-month holding rule, with applicable capital-gains rates; US dividend withholding and foreign tax credits may also matter. Please consult the approved tax guide and a qualified advisor for current rules.
